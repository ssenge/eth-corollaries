\title{Machine-checked algorithmic consequences of a $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm for \#$k$-SAT}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In October 2026 two groups claimed that the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false, and both
formalized their algorithms in Lean. A refuted lower bound gives no algorithm by itself: each
consequence needs a reduction to \#SAT with few variables and, for a uniform bound, small clause
width. We prove such consequences as Lean 4 theorems about Turing machines, on top of Anthropic's
formalized $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm, using only the standard axioms. The results include: counting the
satisfying assignments of CNFs of any width with up to $n\cdot 2^{(\lg n)^{1/4}/(\lg\lg n)^2}$
clauses; counting $k$-colorings for $\lg k \le (\lg n)^{1/6-\theta}$ with one machine; the Max-SAT
spectrum; the density of states of the $q$-state Potts model and the Tutte polynomial on the
hyperbolas $(x-1)(y-1)=q\in\mathbb N$ for graphs with $O(n)$ edges; the parity of the number of
Hamiltonian cycles in graphs with $n(\lg n)^{1/2-\theta}$ edges; the parity of connected solutions
(Steiner trees, connected vertex covers) on dense graphs; randomized algorithms with one-sided error
for Hamiltonian cycle on $O(n)$-edge graphs and for Steiner tree and connected vertex cover on any
graph, via a formalized isolation lemma for arbitrary sets; and Vertex Cover in
$2^{\varepsilon k}\poly(n)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ without a kernel. Each running time is
$2^{\varepsilon n}$ (or $2^{\varepsilon k}\poly(n)$) for every $\varepsilon > 0$. The Hamiltonian
cycle count is checked against Mathlib's definition. Everything is conditional only on the
correctness of Lean and of the statements of Anthropic's formalization; it is not conditional on any
unproved hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) of Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{IP01} says that 3-SAT on $n$
variables needs time $2^{\Omega(n)}$. Much of fine-grained complexity rests on it. In October 2026
two groups claimed that it is false.

\begin{itemize}
\item OpenAI~\cite{openai} claims \#$k$-SAT for every fixed $k$ in time
$(n+m)^{O_k(1)}\cdot 2^{O_k(n\lg\lg n/\sqrt{\lg n})}$, deterministic and in polynomial space. The
core tool is the contraction of Boolean tensor networks with $s$ wires in time
$2^{O(s/\sqrt{\log s})}$.
\item Anthropic~\cite{anthropic} claims \#$k$-SAT with one machine for all $k$ in time
$2^{C\min(k^2 n\lg\lg n/\sqrt{\lg n},\; km/\sqrt{\lg m})}$ with an absolute constant $C$, via
sum-product computations on the clause graph.
\end{itemize}

Both papers are unrefereed and both come with Lean formalizations~\cite{formalmath}. A review led by
Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams is under way; its organizers stress that taking part is not a
certification. If the claims hold, ETH, SETH, \#ETH, Gap-ETH and NSETH fall. The OV conjecture, the
CNF-SAT hypothesis for $\poly(n)$ clauses of unbounded width, the $k$-Clique conjecture and the Set
Cover conjecture still stand. The algorithms are galactic: Anthropic reports no gain over $2^n$
below $n\approx 10^{10}$.

\paragraph{Corollaries are not automatic.} A lower bound that is refuted gives no algorithm. To get a
$2^{o(n)}$ algorithm for a problem $X$ one needs a reduction from $X$ to (\#)SAT with
$o(n\sqrt{\lg n}/\lg\lg n)$ variables and, for the uniform bound, small clause width. Problems in
size-constrained SNP reduce this way~\cite{IPZ01}. Problems with global constraints (connectivity,
orderings, large numbers) and dense pairwise interactions do not. Our results are reductions of this
kind, some standard and some new, with two recurring tools: Cut \& Count~\cite{CNPPRW11} turns
connectivity into a parity of a count, and sparse parity checks~\cite{CIKP08} isolate one solution
without new variables per solution.

\paragraph{What we prove and how.} Every result is a Lean~4 theorem that a single Turing machine
(Mathlib's multi-stack model TM2, finite alphabets) halts on a stated input with a stated output
within a stated number of steps. The theorems are built on Anthropic's Lean development, which proves
its $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm as a theorem; so our results are unconditional theorems of Lean, as sound
as Anthropic's formalization. They use the axioms \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and
\lean{Quot.sound} only. The development has about 10{,}200 lines in 29 files and 43 main theorems,
whose axioms are printed by a check file. Source: \url{https://github.com/ssenge/eth-corollaries}.

Lean cannot check that a formal statement says what is intended. The definitions behind the
statements must be read by hand: ours (\lean{numHom} and \lean{hmat}, checked against Mathlib's $G\to_g H$, \lean{numHamCycles}, \lean{spectrum}, \lean{pottsSpec},
\lean{mono}, \lean{comps}, \lean{tutte}, \lean{Seed}, the input codes) and Anthropic's (the TM2 model
and their statement files). For one of ours, the number of Hamiltonian cycles, we prove agreement with
Mathlib's independent definition (Section~\ref{sec:mathlib}).

literature check for the entries marked ``new''.

\section{The interface}

We use three parts of Anthropic's development as black boxes.

\begin{theorem}[\SIZED, Anthropic]\label{thm:sized}
For every width $w$ and $\delta>0$ there are a machine $\SIZED_w$ (a polynomial-time stepped
computation) and $c$ such that for all $N$, $s\le N$ and every CNF $\psi$ on $N$ variables with clauses
of at most $w$ literals, $\SIZED_w$ on $(1^N, s, \psi)$ returns the number $\mathrm{cnt}_s(\psi)$ of
satisfying assignments with exactly $s$ true variables, within $c\cdot 2^{\delta N}$ steps of size at
most $c(N+|\psi|+2)^c$.
\end{theorem}

The number of clauses of $\psi$ only enters the polynomial factor. This is what makes dense graphs
possible whenever the variables are the vertices.

\paragraph{Stepped machines, trees and the host.} Anthropic's machines are \emph{stepped}: a
computation is cut into polynomial-time steps, and a theorem turns a stepped machine that runs in
$T$ steps of state size $S$ into a TM2 machine with time $q(T+1)(|x|+S+1)^q$. A depth-first walk
over the tree of a step function $\sigma\to\mathrm{Option}(\sigma\times\sigma)$ that sums a stepped
computation over the leaves is again stepped (\lean{walkSum}), with a bound on the number of leaves
when every path has at most $D$ steps and at most $T/q$ ``heavy'' steps. The host theorem
\lean{tm_of_stepped} gives time $\lfloor C2^{\delta n}\rfloor$ from $c\,2^{\delta n/2}$ steps; we add
an FPT version \lean{tm_fpt} with time $\lfloor C(n+2)^C 2^{\delta p}\rfloor$ for a parameter $p$.

\paragraph{Encodings.} A graph on $n$ vertices is coded as $1^n0$ followed by its adjacency matrix,
a number $t$ in unary as $1^t0$, a CNF as $1^n0$ followed by its clauses, each literal in
$\lfloor\lg 2n\rfloor+1$ bits.

\section{Counting}

\subsection{CNF of any width (A1, A1$'$)}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{cnf_counts}]
For every $D$ and $\delta>0$ one machine counts the satisfying assignments of every CNF with $n$
variables and at most $Dn$ clauses, of any width, within $C2^{\delta n}$ steps.
\end{theorem}

This is the width--density duality of Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{CIP06} combined with
$\neg$ETH. The literal $(u,b)$ becomes the variable $2u$ or $2u+1$, with pair clauses making them
complementary, so every clause is positive. Anthropic's clause-shortening tree from their
Dominating Set corollary cuts clauses to width $w$; we replace its invariant (which needs at most
$n$ clauses) by one for $M=Dn$ clauses. The tree has at most $2^{(2/w)\lg(ew(D+2))n}$ leaves, and
\SIZED{} counts at each leaf.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{cnf_grow}]
One machine: for every $\delta>0$ there is $n_0$ such that for every CNF on $n\ge n_0$ variables with
at most $n\cdot 2^{(\lg n)^{1/4}/(\lg\lg n)^2}$ clauses of any width, it outputs the number of
satisfying assignments within $2^{\delta n}$ steps.
\end{theorem}

The width is now read off the node: with $\ell=\lfloor\lg 2n\rfloor$ and $L=\lfloor\lg\ell\rfloor$,
$w=2^{\lfloor L/4\rfloor-\lfloor\lg L\rfloor}+1\approx\ell^{1/4}/L$. The leaves are counted by
Anthropic's uniform machine, whose exponent $Cw^2N\lg\lg N/\sqrt{\lg N}$ is at most $32CN/L=o(n)$
since $w^2L^2\le 16\sqrt\ell$. The tree coefficient is at most $(8+6\sqrt w)/w+8/(L-1)\to 0$ for
$\lg D\le 4w/(L-1)$. The method stops at density $n2^{o((\lg n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\lg\lg n})}$.

\subsection{Colorings with a growing number of colors (A2)}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{colorings_subexp}]
One machine counts the proper $k$-colorings of an $n$-vertex graph, given $k$ and the graph, within
$2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $0<\theta<1/6$, $n\ge n_0(\theta,\varepsilon)$
and all $2\le k$ with $\lg k\le(\lg n)^{1/6-\theta}$.
\end{theorem}

Colors are written in $b=\lceil\lg k\rceil$ bits; the CNF has $nb+4$ variables, width $2b$, and one
satisfying assignment per proper coloring. The uniform bound with width $2b$ and $N=nb$ variables is
$O(n(\lg n)^{-2\theta})$ in the exponent.

\paragraph{Homomorphisms (N5).} The same encoding counts homomorphisms into a target that is part of
the input. Let $H$ be a relation on $\{0,\dots,h-1\}$ (loops allowed, not necessarily symmetric),
given by its $h^2$ entries, and let $\mathrm{hom}(G,H)$ be the number of maps $f:V(G)\to[h]$ with
$H(f(u),f(v))$ for every edge $uv$.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{hom_subexp}, \lean{hom_subexp_graph}]
One machine, given $h$ in unary, the matrix of $H$ and an $n$-vertex graph $G$, outputs
$\mathrm{hom}(G,H)$ within $2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $0<\theta<1/6$,
$n\ge n_0(\theta,\varepsilon)$ and all $h\ge2$ with $\lg h\le(\lg n)^{1/6-\theta}$. For a simple
graph $H$ the output is the number of Mathlib homomorphisms $G\to_g H$ (\lean{numHom_simple}).
\end{theorem}

With $b=\lceil\lg h\rceil$ bits per vertex, every edge $uv$ of $G$ and every pair $(a,c)$ with
$H(a,c)$ false give the clause ``not both $u\mapsto a$ and $v\mapsto c$'' of width $2b$
(\lean{homB}, \lean{cntN_homB}). The variables and the width are those of A2; only the number of
clauses grows, to at most $n^2h^2+O(nh)$, which the uniform bound does not see beyond a polynomial
factor. For $H=K_h$ this is A2 (\lean{numColorings_eq}); for $H$ an edge with a loop at one end it
counts independent sets. Under ETH, deciding whether $G\to H$ exists takes $|V(H)|^{\Omega(|V(G)|)}$
time in general~\cite{CFGKMPS17}.

\subsection{Spectra (A4) and the Potts model (N3)}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{maxsat_spectrum}]
For every width $r$, density $D$ and $\delta>0$, one machine outputs, given $t$ and a CNF $\varphi$
with clauses of at most $r$ literals and at most $Dn$ clauses, the number of assignments that satisfy
exactly $t$ clauses of $\varphi$, within $C2^{\delta n}$ steps.
\end{theorem}

On $2n+m$ variables ($x_u$, $\bar x_u$, and $y_j\leftrightarrow C_j$) the satisfying assignments with
exactly $n+t$ true variables are the assignments with exactly $t$ satisfied clauses. This covers the
Max-Cut spectrum and the density of states of the $\pm1$ Ising model on $O(n)$-edge graphs.

For a graph $G$ and $q$ colors let $N_j$ be the number of colorings $\sigma:V\to[q]$ with exactly $j$
monochromatic edges, $k(A)$ the number of components of $(V,A)$, and
$T(G;x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}(x-1)^{k(A)-k(E)}(y-1)^{|A|+k(A)-n}$ the Tutte polynomial.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{potts_spectrum}, \lean{potts_fk}, \lean{tutte_hyperbola}, \lean{tutte_tm}]
For every $q\ge1$, $D$ and $\delta>0$, one machine outputs $N_j$ given $G$ with at most $Dn$ edges and
$j$, within $C2^{\delta n}$ steps. Moreover, in every commutative ring,
$\sum_j N_j(1+v)^j=\sum_{A\subseteq E}q^{k(A)}v^{|A|}$, and in every field, if $(x-1)(y-1)=q$ and
$x,y\ne1$, then $(x-1)^{k(E)}(y-1)^nT(G;x,y)=\sum_j N_jy^j$.
\end{theorem}

The CNF has variables $x_{vq+c}$ ($v$ has color $c$) and $y_i$ (edge $i$ is monochromatic), $nq+m$
in all, and width $q+3$. Its satisfying assignments with $n+j$ true variables are the colorings with
$j$ monochromatic edges. The identities are the Fortuin--Kasteleyn expansion~\cite{FK72}: expand
$\prod_e(1+v[e\text{ mono}])$ over $A\subseteq E$ and count the colorings constant on the components of
$(V,A)$, which are $q^{k(A)}$ many. This gives the chromatic polynomial at $q$ (the point $(1-q,0)$)
and the flow polynomial at $q$ (the point $(0,1-q)$). Under \#ETH, Dell et al.~\cite{DHMTW14} prove
lower bounds of the form $2^{\Omega(n/\mathrm{polylog}\,n)}$ for such evaluations on sparse graphs.

\section{Connectivity through parity}

\subsection{Hamiltonian cycles (A5a, A5a$'$)}

Let $\mathrm{hc}(G)$ be the number of connected spanning subgraphs of $G$ with all degrees 2.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{hc_parity_subexp}, \lean{hc_parity_sparse}]
For every $\Delta$, one machine decides whether a graph with all degrees at most $\Delta$ has an odd
number of Hamiltonian cycles, within $2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps for $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)$. One machine
decides it for all graphs with at most $n(\lg n)^{1/2-\theta}$ edges, within $2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps
for $n\ge n_0(\theta,\varepsilon)$.
\end{theorem}

The Cut \& Count CNF~\cite{CNPPRW11} has an edge bit per edge and a side bit per vertex, and says
that every vertex is on exactly two chosen edges, the ends of a chosen edge share a side, and vertex
0 has side 0. A 2-factor with $c$ cycles has $2^{c-1}$ side maps, which is odd exactly when $c=1$.
For bounded degree the formula has width $\Delta+3$; for any degree a sequential counter with two bits
per (edge, end) replaces the cardinality constraints, giving width 3 and $5m+n+2$ variables. Anthropic's
bound $2^{9cN\lg\lg N/\sqrt{\lg N}}$ is then $2^{o(n)}$ for $m=o(n\sqrt{\lg n}/\lg\lg n)$.

\subsection{Connected solutions on dense graphs (N2)}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{conn_parity_tm}, \lean{cvc_parity_tm}]
For every width $r$, exponent $D$ and $\delta>0$ one machine, given a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices of any
density, $1\le\ell\le n$ and a CNF $\varphi$ on the vertices with clauses of at most $r$ literals and at
most $(n+2)^D$ clauses, outputs the parity of the number of $X\subseteq V$ with $|X|=\ell$,
$X\models\varphi$ and $G[X]$ connected, within $C2^{\delta n}$ steps.
\end{theorem}

The variables are a vertex bit $x_v$, a side bit $s_v$ and a complement bit $\bar s_v$; the clauses
are those of $\varphi$, $\bar s_v=\neg s_v$, $s_v\to x_v$ and $s_u=s_v$ on edges inside $X$. The
satisfying assignments with $n+\ell$ true variables are the pairs $(X,s)$, $2^{\mathrm{cc}(G[X])}$ per
$X$, and half their number is odd exactly when $G[X]$ is connected. The formula has $3n$ variables
whatever the number of edges. With $\varphi$ the unit clauses of a terminal set this counts the vertex
sets of Steiner trees on $\ell$ vertices; with $\varphi$ the edge clauses, connected vertex covers.

\section{Isolation and one-sided error}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{isolation}; Calabro, Impagliazzo, Kabanets, Paturi~\cite{CIKP08}]
For every $\delta>0$ there are $k,t$ such that for $M\ge M_0(\delta)$ and every nonempty
$S\subseteq\{0,1\}^M$, at least a $2^{-\delta M}$ fraction of the seeds leaves exactly one point of
$S$. A seed is $3M+3$ parity checks on $k$ coordinates, a number of them to use, and for every
coordinate a value in $\{0,\dots,t-1\}$ and a bit (the coordinate is fixed to the bit when the value
is 0).
\end{theorem}

\cite{CIKP08} states this for solution sets of $k$-CNFs, but the proof uses only that $S$ is
nonempty. A check on $k$ coordinates becomes a chain of $k$ auxiliary variables
$a_l=a_{l-1}\oplus(c_l\wedge x_{j_l})$ in clauses of width at most 3, determined by the main
variables; so isolation keeps the number of variables linear, unlike Valiant--Vazirani hashing.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{ham_randomized}, \lean{conn_randomized}]
(a) For every $D,\delta,\varepsilon>0$ there are $k,t,n_0$ and one machine that, on a graph with
$n\ge n_0$ vertices and at most $Dn$ edges and a seed $\omega$, halts within $2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps
with a bit that is 0 for every seed if $G$ has no Hamiltonian cycle, and 1 for at least a
$2^{-\delta n}$ fraction of the seeds if it has one.
(b) The same, for graphs of any density, holds for the existence of a vertex set of size $\ell$ that
satisfies a CNF $\varphi$ as in N2 and induces a connected subgraph; in particular for Steiner trees
on $\ell$ vertices and connected vertex covers of size $\ell$, within $C2^{\varepsilon n}$ steps.
\end{theorem}

For (a), the checks act on the edge bits; the count modulo 2 of the Hamiltonian cycles passing the
checks is odd for a $2^{-\delta m}$ fraction of seeds. For (b), the checks act on the vertex bits, and
every chain bit gets a complement bit so that the number of true variables stays fixed, as \SIZED{}
requires. The machines read the seed from the input, so one machine serves every $\delta$. Repeating
with $2^{2\delta n}$ independent seeds gives error $e^{-2^{\delta n}}$; this step is not formalized.

\section{Vertex Cover parameterized by the solution size (A3)}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{vc_fpt}]
For every $\varepsilon>0$, one machine, given a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices (of any density) and $k\le
n$, decides whether $G$ has a vertex cover of at most $k$ vertices within $C(n+2)^C2^{\varepsilon k}$
steps.
\end{theorem}

Cai and Juedes~\cite{CJ03} showed that such an algorithm refutes ETH; the converse with a linear
kernel is folklore. We avoid the kernel. While the budget $k'$ is positive and a vertex $v$ has at
least $d$ distinct neighbours, branch: $v$ is in the cover (budget $k'-1$) or all of $N(v)$ is (budget
$k'-|N(v)|\le k'-d$). A node is good iff one child is (\lean{good_step}). Along a path the budget
drops by 1 or by at least $d$, so with Anthropic's tree bound the tree has at most
$\sum_{j\le(k+d)/d}\binom{k+d}{j}\le 2^{\varepsilon'(k+d)}$ leaves once $(1/d)\lg(ed)\le\varepsilon'$
(\lean{leaves_le}). At a leaf all degrees are below $d$, so a cover of size $k'$ leaves at most $k'd$
vertices on the edges (\lean{card_V_le}); \SIZED{} decides the leaf on these vertices, in
$2^{\delta kd}$ steps, and $\delta=\varepsilon/(4d)$ makes it $2^{\varepsilon k/4}$.

\section{Checking a definition against Mathlib}\label{sec:mathlib}

Mathlib defines Hamiltonian cycles as closed walks that are cycles and visit every vertex once
(\lean{Walk.IsHamiltonianCycle}), and \lean{SimpleGraph.IsHamiltonian}.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{numHamCycles_pos_iff}, \lean{numHamCycles_eq}]
For $n\ne1$, $\mathrm{hc}(G)>0$ iff $G$ is Hamiltonian in Mathlib's sense. Moreover $\mathrm{hc}(G)$
equals the number of edge sets of Mathlib Hamiltonian cycles of $G$.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses Mathlib's result that a connected graph with all degrees 2 is a single cycle through
its vertices. With it, (a) of the previous section is stated directly for \lean{G.IsHamiltonian}
(\lean{ham_randomized_mathlib}).

\section{Discussion}

The results mark where the claimed algorithms reach. The variable count is the currency: $O(n)$
variables give $2^{\varepsilon n}$, $n\cdot r(n)$ variables with $r=o(\sqrt{\lg n}/\lg\lg n)$ still
give $2^{o(n)}$, and the clause width must stay below about $(\lg n)^{1/4}$ for the uniform bound.
Open directions: deterministic decision versions of A5b and N2$'$; Hamiltonian cycle and Steiner tree
with more edges; the Tutte polynomial off the integer hyperbolas; Nonblocker and other
linear-kernel problems; and the Tier B problems of the repository notes (dense Max-Cut, the permanent,
CNF-SAT with $\poly(n)$ clauses, planar problems below $2^{O(\sqrt n)}$, Subset Sum), where the
refutations remove a lower bound but no reduction is known.
